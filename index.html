Understanding heart disease

What goes wrong inside the heart

“Heart disease” is an umbrella for several conditions. The one this project focuses on is coronary artery disease, the most common cause of heart attack.

Coronary artery disease
The arteries that feed the heart muscle narrow as plaque builds up inside them.
Arrhythmia
The heart beats too fast, too slow or unevenly.
Heart failure
The heart cannot pump enough blood for the body’s needs.
Valve disease
A heart valve does not open or close properly.

Risk factors

What raises the risk

Factors you can change

  • High blood pressure
  • High cholesterol
  • Tobacco use
  • Diabetes and high blood sugar
  • Physical inactivity
  • Unhealthy diet and excess weight
  • Harmful use of alcohol

Factors you cannot change

  • Age (risk rises over the years)
  • Sex (men tend to develop it earlier)
  • Family history of early heart disease

The predictor in Module 3 uses the measurable factors above together with exercise-test results and symptoms.

Warning signs of a heart attack

  • Pain or discomfort in the centre of the chest
  • Pain in the arms, left shoulder, elbows, jaw or back
  • Shortness of breath
  • Nausea or vomiting, light-headedness, a cold sweat, or pale skin

Symptoms can be mild or different in some people, including women. A risk score cannot tell you whether something is happening right now.

If you think you or someone near you is having a heart attack, call your local emergency number straight away (for example 112, 911 or 999) instead of using this site.

How prediction works

From measurements to a risk estimate

  1. In

    Measurements go in

    Age, blood pressure, cholesterol, exercise-test results and symptoms are entered as numbers and choices.

  2. Model

    The model compares

    A machine learning model learned from patient records where the outcome was known. It weighs each measurement and how they combine.

  3. Out

    A risk estimate comes out

    The result is a score and a band. It estimates how likely heart disease is. It does not diagnose it.

The data behind the model

One row per patient, one column per measurement

Prediction models learn from tables of patient records. The final column records whether heart disease was present. Public collections such as the UCI Heart Disease data use this layout, with measurements like these.

Limits

What a prediction cannot do

It estimates, it does not diagnose.A score shows likelihood. Only a clinician can confirm or rule out disease.

It only knows its training data.A model reflects the patterns in the records it learned from, and can be wrong for people unlike them.

It does not replace an examination.Symptoms, history and tests carry far more information than a handful of inputs.

Serving

What happens when you press predict

  1. The user fills in the form. The browser sends the values to the Flask app.
  2. Flask checks that every value is present and in a sensible range.
  3. The values are arranged into an array in the same column order used during training.
  4. The loaded model returns the probability of heart disease.
  5. Flask sends the result back and the page updates the risk display.

Illustrative Flask route

@app.route("/predict", methods=["POST"])
def predict():
    data = request.get_json()
    row = np.array([[data[name] for name in FEATURES]])
    risk = model.predict_proba(row)[0][1]
    return jsonify(risk=round(float(risk) * 100, 1))

Evaluation

How a screening model is judged

Accuracy alone can hide problems. For a health screen, missing someone who is at risk costs more than a false alarm, so recall gets special attention.

Accuracy
The share of all predictions that are correct.
Precision
Of the people flagged as at risk, how many really were.
Recall
Of the people who really had the disease, how many were flagged.
ROC-AUC
How well the model ranks at-risk people above healthy ones across every threshold.
